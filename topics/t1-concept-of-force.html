⚡What is a Force?

In Chapter 4 you learned to describe motion — position, velocity and acceleration. But you did not ask: what actually makes an object move, stop or change direction? The answer of this chapter is force. A force is simply an effort of push or pull acting on an object.

📌
Definition of force: Force is an external effort (a push or a pull) which can move a stationary object, stop a moving object, change its speed or direction, or change its shape — or try to do these things.

⚽ Kicking a ball

The ball was at rest; your foot pushes it and it starts moving.

🏏 Striking a ball

The bat applies force and changes the direction of the moving ball.

🍋 Squeezing a lemon

The force of your fingers changes the shape of the lemon.

⭐ The five effects of a force

  • A force can move a stationary body (kick a ball).
  • A force can stop a moving body (catch the ball, apply brakes).
  • A force can change the speed of a moving body (hit the ball harder).
  • A force can change the direction of motion (bat turns the ball).
  • A force can change the shape (and size) of a body (squeeze clay, spring).

The word “tries to” is important. If you push a heavy wall, you apply a force, but the wall may not move — your force only tries to produce motion. So a force need not always cause motion.

🧭Force needs direction — it is a Vector Quantity

Whenever we describe a force we also say its direction — friction acts opposite to motion, magnets repel or attract, the Earth pulls objects towards itself, and a liquid pushes a floating ball upwards. A quantity that needs both magnitude (amount) and direction is called a vector quantity — like displacement, velocity and acceleration from Chapter 4.

FORCE IS A VECTORForce = magnitude (how strong) + direction (which way)
💡
If the magnitude changes, or the direction changes, or both change, then the effect of the force also changes.

⚖️ The SI unit of force — the newton (N)

The SI unit of force is the newton. In books, when we write the full unit name we write it in small letters — newton (never “Newton”), but its symbol is the capital letter N. This rule is followed because the unit is named after the great scientist Isaac Newton.

🔸 Contact forces

Require touching — muscular force, force of friction, force of a spring or bat.

🔹 Non-contact forces

Act without touching — gravitational, magnetic and electrostatic force.

🔬
Four fundamental forces of nature: (i) gravitational force, (ii) electromagnetic force, (iii) strong nuclear force and (iv) weak nuclear force. All other forces we feel are made from these. You will learn them in higher classes.

🌀How do we measure the magnitude of a force?

A spring balance is used to measure force. You may already know it is used to measure weight (the gravitational force on an object). If you pull the free end of a spring balance, it measures the force of your pull through how much its spring stretches.

🔎
Thread of curiosity: the smallest forces we can directly feel are about a few millinewtons (10⁻³ N) — like a light touch. But with special instruments, scientists can measure forces as small as yoctonewtons (10⁻²⁴ N, as of 2026)!
REMEMBERThe magnitude of a force = its strength  ·  unit: newton (N)

?Do two objects always have to touch each other for a force to act?🎧▾

No. Forces that need touching are called contact forces (muscular force, friction). But non-contact forces act even without touching — the Earth pulls a falling apple (gravitational force), a magnet pulls iron from a distance (magnetic force), and a comb rubbed on dry hair attracts paper bits (electrostatic force).

?What is the SI unit of force and what is its symbol?🎧▾

The SI unit of force is the newton, written in full with a small letter n. Its symbol is the capital N. One newton is a small force — holding a 100 g mass in your palm needs about 1 N of force.

?Which instrument measures force and how does it work?🎧▾

A spring balance measures force. When you pull its hook, the spring inside stretches. The more the force, the more the spring stretches, and the scale shows the magnitude of the force. It is the same instrument we use to measure weight.

?Why is direction so important while describing a force?🎧▾

Because force is a vector quantity — it has magnitude (strength) and direction. A 5 N push to the right and a 5 N push to the left are different forces. If magnitude or direction changes, the effect of the force changes too.

1Short Answer: What is force? State its SI unit and explain why force is called a vector quantity.Book · Short Q1▾ Answer
Answer —

A force is a physical quantity that can make an object move from rest, change its speed and direction of motion, and even change its shape. The SI unit of force is the newton (full name in small letters, symbol N). One newton is the force that produces an acceleration of 1 m/s² on a 1 kg object. Force is a vector quantity because we need both its magnitude (strength) and its direction to describe it completely — just like position, displacement, velocity and acceleration. If the magnitude or direction of a force changes, its effect changes.
