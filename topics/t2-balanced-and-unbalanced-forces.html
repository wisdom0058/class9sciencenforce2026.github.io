⚖️ 6.2 Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

Many forces act together on an object — when do they cancel out, and when does a net force appear?

⚖️When many forces act together

In real life, more than one force usually acts on an object. Example: you push a box on the floor — but the force of friction also acts on it, in the opposite direction. A ball floating on water has two forces: gravity pulls it down, and the buoyant force of water pushes it up.

📦 Box on floor

Your push forward + friction backward.

🏐 Ball floating

Gravity (down) + buoyant force (up).

🪢 Tug of war

Two teams pull the rope in opposite directions.

So what decides the motion of an object? The answer is one simple rule —

KEY RULEThe motion of an object depends only on its NET FORCE

The net force is the single force that gives the same effect as all the forces acting together.

🧮Balanced vs Unbalanced forces — the net-force rules

🤝 Balanced forces

Two forces that are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction are called balanced forces. Balanced forces cancel out — their net force is zero, so they cause no acceleration. In a tug of war where both teams pull with equal force, the rope simply does not move.

🏃 Unbalanced forces

When the forces are not balanced, a non-zero net force acts on the object, and the object accelerates in the direction of that net force. If one team pulls harder, the rope moves towards the stronger team.

↔️ Opposite directions

Net force = difference of the two magnitudes; direction = direction of the larger force.

➡️ Same direction

Net force = sum of the magnitudes; direction = same as both forces. (Two people push a stalled car together.)

🔭
Ready to go beyond: if forces act at an angle (not parallel/opposite) you will learn in higher classes how to combine them. Also, two equal and opposite forces on the two ends of a tap or handlebar do not cancel in effect — they make the object rotate!

✏️ Worked Example 6.1 (from your book)

Two forces of 10 N and 6 N act on a block on a table as shown. Find the magnitude and direction of the net force in each case.
(a) Both act towards the right: Net force = 10 N + 6 N = 16 N towards the right.
(b) 10 N right, 6 N left: Net force = 10 N − 6 N = 4 N towards the right (towards the larger force).
(c) 10 N left, 6 N right: Net force = 10 N − 6 N = 4 N towards the left.

?What are balanced forces?🎧▾

Two forces that are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction are called balanced forces. Their net force is zero, so they do not change the state of motion — a resting object stays at rest (no acceleration). Example: in a tug of war, two equal teams pulling a rope that does not move.

?What is net force?🎧▾

When two or more forces act on an object and they are not balanced, the single resultant force that replaces all of them is called the net force. It decides the motion of the object — direction of motion = direction of net force.

?If two forces act in the same direction, how do we find the net force?🎧▾

We add the two magnitudes. The net force acts in the same direction as the two forces. Example: two people push a stalled car with 40 N and 30 N forward → net force = 70 N forward.

?If two forces act in opposite directions, how do we find the net force?🎧▾

We subtract the smaller force from the larger force. The net force acts in the direction of the larger force. Example: 15 N right and 9 N left → net force = 6 N to the right.

?Can balanced forces be acting on an object that is already moving?🎧▾

Yes! Balanced forces mean the net force is zero — the object's acceleration is zero. So it can move with a constant velocity (steady speed, straight line) without any net force, as we will see in Newton's first law.

?What happens when equal and opposite forces act on the two ends of a tap or a steering handle?🎧▾

They do not simply cancel; this pair makes the object rotate. You will study turning effects (torque/moment) in higher classes. For now remember: motion depends on net force, but equal-opposite pairs on an extended body can turn it.

?What is the normal force acting on an object placed on a surface?🎧▾

The normal force is the force exerted by a surface on an object placed on it; it acts perpendicular (normal) to the surface in the upward direction. For an object resting on a flat table, the normal force balances the object's weight, so the two are balanced and the object stays at rest. (“Normal” in science means perpendicular.)

📗Book Questions & Answers on this topic

Pause-and-Ponder and Book questions for §6.2 — tap to reveal the answer.

1Pause and Ponder: A weightlifter lifts a barbell. List two forces acting on the barbell. Are these forces balanced when the barbell is held steady?NCERT · P&P Q1▾ Answer
Answer —

The two forces on the barbell are: (1) the downward gravitational force (weight) exerted by the Earth, and (2) the upward force applied by the weightlifter. When the barbell is held completely steady, the barbell is not accelerating, so these two equal-and-opposite forces are balanced (net force = 0).

2Pause and Ponder: Two players R and S are arm-wrestling. When the arms tilt forward, are the forces exerted by the players balanced? If not, who exerted the larger force?NCERT · P&P Q2▾ Answer
Answer —

When the arms tilt to the front direction, the forces are not balanced — a net force is acting. The player whose hand is moving forward (winning that instant) has exerted the larger force; the losing side's force is smaller. (Because the arms are moving, the net force cannot be zero.)

3Exercise: Two blocks P and Q are shown. Forces 4 N and 5 N act in opposite directions on P, while Q moves with constant velocity. Which statement is correct?NCERT · Exercise Q3▾ Answer
Answer —

(i) P experiences a net force and Q does not experience a net force.
On P the two opposite forces are unequal (5 N and 4 N), so net force on P = 1 N. Block Q is moving with constant velocity, which means its acceleration is zero, so the net force on Q is zero.

4Exercise: In a snake-boat race (Vallam kali, Kerala) 100 oarsmen row together — 95 row backwards to propel the boat forward but 5 mistakenly row in the opposite direction. Each applies 200 N. What is the net force on the boat?NCERT · Exercise Q4▾ Answer
Answer —

Force of 95 oarsmen in the forward direction = 95 × 200 = 19,000 N.
Force of 5 oarsmen in the backward direction = 5 × 200 = 1,000 N.
Net force = 19,000 − 1,000 = 18,000 N forward (drag and air friction ignored).

5Short Answer: Distinguish between balanced and unbalanced forces with one daily-life example of each.Book · Short Q2▾ Answer
Answer —

Balanced forces: equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, acting on the same object; net force is zero and the object does not accelerate (stays at rest or keeps constant velocity). Example: two equal teams in a tug of war — the rope does not move.
Unbalanced forces: not equal, so a non-zero net force appears and the object accelerates in the direction of the larger force. Example: when one team pulls harder, the rope moves toward that team.

6Short Answer: Two forces of 15 N and 9 N act on a block. Find the net force when (i) both act in the same direction, (ii) they act in opposite directions.Book · Short Q3▾ Answer
Answer —

(i) Same direction → net force = 15 + 9 = 24 N in the direction of both forces.
(ii) Opposite directions → net force = 15 − 9 = 6 N in the direction of the larger (15 N) force.
