🧲 6.3 The Force of Friction

The hidden force that always opposes motion — why objects slow down and stop on their own.

🧲The Force of Friction — always present

Push a box lying on the floor. Sometimes it does not move at all, and you must push harder. Why? Because between the bottom of the box and the floor there is an opposing force — the force of friction.

DEFINITIONFriction is the force that opposes the motion (or tendency to move) of one surface over another

Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion (or the intended motion). The box starts moving only when your push is larger than the force of friction.

🧱 All the forces on a pushed box

  • Applied force — your push, in the direction of motion.
  • Force of friction — opposite to the motion.
  • Gravitational force (weight) — straight down.
  • Normal force — the upward push of the floor, perpendicular to the surface (it balances the weight).
🚲
When you stop pedalling a bicycle it slows down and stops. When you stop pushing a ball it also stops. This does not mean you must always push to keep something moving — it means you must push only to balance the force of friction so the net force stays zero.

🔬Investigating friction — what does it depend on?

🔎 Activity 6.1 — coins, rubber band and different surfaces

Stretch a rubber band equally each time and use it to launch a stack of coins on different surfaces: wooden table top, cemented floor, laminated table and polished marble/tile.

  • On a smooth surface the coin stack travels a larger distance and its velocity decreases more slowly.
  • On a rough surface it stops after a shorter distance.

🔎 Activity 6.2 — measuring friction with a spring balance

Pull a wooden block with a spring balance on each surface. The reading when the block just starts to move tells the magnitude of the friction. The surface on which the coins travelled the most gives the smallest reading (least friction).

🧠
Conclusion: the force of friction depends on the nature of the surfaces in contact — rougher surface ⇒ larger friction ⇒ object stops sooner.

🚀 Thought experiment (think like Galileo!)

Imagine a perfectly smooth floor with zero friction. If you push an object, it will never slow down — it will keep moving forever with constant velocity. This idea leads directly to Newton's first law (next topic).

⚙️Why friction happens + types of friction

🧱 Causes of friction

  • Roughness / interlocking: no surface is perfectly smooth — under a microscope even shiny surfaces have tiny “hills and valleys” that interlock and oppose sliding.
  • Adhesion: molecules of the two touching surfaces attract each other slightly and resist sliding.

📊 Types of friction

TypeWhen it actsExample
Static frictionObject is not yet moving — friction is self-adjusting and balances your push up to a maximum called limiting friction.Pushing a heavy box that does not move.
Sliding (kinetic) frictionOne surface slides over the other.Block sliding on a table.
Rolling frictionOne body rolls over the other — much smaller than sliding friction.Wheels, ball bearings, roller skates.
Fluid friction (drag)A body moves through a fluid (liquid or gas).Swimming, flying, air resistance.

📏 Limiting friction

The maximum static friction just before the body starts to slide. Static friction keeps increasing to balance your push until this limit.

💡 Order

Limiting static > sliding friction > rolling friction — that is why wheels were a great invention!

🌟
Interesting: friction depends on (i) how hard the surfaces press together (weight on a flat floor) and (ii) the nature of the surfaces. It does not depend on the area of contact (for the same weight).

🛠️Friction — friend or enemy? Increase it or reduce it!

😇 Friction is a “necessary evil”

  • It helps us walk and run — we push the ground backwards and friction pushes us forward (you will meet this again in the third law!).
  • It lets vehicles run and stop — tyres grip the road; brakes use friction to stop wheels.
  • Nails and screws hold walls; matchsticks light up; we can write because the pen tip grips paper.
  • But friction also causes wear and tear, wastes energy as heat, and causes forest fires when dry branches rub.

📈 How we INCREASE friction

Make surfaces rough · grooves on soles and treads on tyres for grip · rough matchbox side · press surfaces harder.

📉 How we REDUCE friction

Polishing · oil / grease (lubricants) · fine powder (talcum on carrom board, graphite in machines) · ball bearings · streamlined shapes · magnetic levitation.

🌊 Fluid friction and streamlining

A streamlined shape lets air or water flow around a body with the least drag — ships, aeroplanes, cars, rockets, birds and fishes are streamlined. Interesting fact: meteors enter the atmosphere so fast that air friction makes them burn (that is why spaceships carry heat shields).

🧠
Think: why is it easy to slip on a wet polished floor or on ice? Because water/ice makes the surface very smooth — friction becomes very small, so your feet cannot grip.

🎬Demo Lab — same push, different surfaces 🎬

Choose a surface and push the coin stack. The smoother the surface, the smaller the friction and the farther the stack slides.

?What is the force of friction and in which direction does it act?🎧▾

The force of friction is the resisting force that arises between two surfaces in contact when one body moves (or tries to move) over another. It always acts in the direction opposite to the motion of the object — push a box forward and friction pulls it backward.

?How does the nature of the surface affect friction?🎧▾

Rougher surfaces give more friction, smoother surfaces give less. With the same initial push, an object travels a larger distance on a polished (smooth) surface and a shorter distance on a rough surface, because friction is smaller on smooth surfaces.

?Why is it difficult to walk on a wet polished floor or on ice?🎧▾

On wet floors or ice the force of friction between your feet and the surface is very small. While walking, friction is what pushes you forward when you push the ground backwards. With little friction, your foot slips and you fall.

?Why does a moving object stop when we stop pushing it? Doesn't the first law say motion continues?🎧▾

A very good doubt! In the real world friction is always present. It is the friction force — not the absence of your push — that brings the object to rest. The first law talks about a situation with zero net force. To keep an object moving at steady speed on a real floor, we apply a force exactly equal to friction so the net force is zero.

?What is limiting (static) friction?🎧▾

Static friction is self-adjusting — it keeps balancing your applied force so the object does not move. The maximum value of static friction just before the body starts sliding is called limiting friction. After motion begins, sliding friction acts, which is smaller.

?List the ways to reduce friction and the ways to increase it.🎧▾

Reduce: polishing surfaces, applying oil or grease (lubrication), sprinkling fine powder, using ball or roller bearings, and giving streamlined shapes. Increase: making surfaces rougher and making grooves/treads on soles and tyres.

?Is friction always harmful?🎧▾

No — friction is called a necessary evil. Without friction we could not walk, drive, hold objects, write, light a match or stop vehicles. But it also causes wear, tear and wasted energy. So we increase it where we need grip and reduce it in machines where it wastes energy.

When a body moves through a liquid or a gas, the fluid exerts a frictional force called drag. A streamlined shape lets the fluid flow around the body with minimum drag — that is why ships, planes, cars, birds and fish are streamlined.

📗Book Questions & Answers on this topic

Source-book questions for §6.3 Force of Friction.

1Short Answer: What is the force of friction? In which direction does it act on a moving object? How does the nature of the surface affect it?Book · Short Q4▾ Answer
Answer —

The force of friction arises between two surfaces in contact when one object moves or tends to move over the other, and it opposes that relative motion. It always acts opposite to the direction of motion. The force of friction depends on the nature of the surfaces in contact — a rougher surface (cemented floor) gives more friction than a smoother surface (polished marble), so objects travel a shorter distance on rough surfaces.

2Exercise: A table is moved across the floor with a horizontal force F at a constant velocity. How much is the frictional force exerted by the floor on the table?NCERT · Exercise Q1▾ Answer
Answer —

Since the table moves with constant velocity, its acceleration is zero. From Newton's first law, the net force on it must be zero. Therefore the force of friction must exactly equal the applied force F (and act in the opposite direction).

3What if…: the force of friction disappears from the world. How will the motion of objects be affected?NCERT · Think▾ Answer
Answer —

If friction vanished, a moving object would never slow down on its own — it would keep moving with the same velocity forever. Walking, running, driving, stopping vehicles, holding objects, writing and lighting matches would become impossible, because all of these need friction. We would slip and fall constantly. So friction, though an evil at times, is a very necessary force!
