🔗Forces acting on a system of objects

So far we applied the laws to one object. But can we use them for two or more connected objects? Consider two boxes of masses m₁ and m₂ on a frictionless surface, connected by a string. A force F pulls Box 1 to the right.

🧵
The string pulls Box 2 with a force called tension T, and by the third law Box 2 pulls Box 1 back with the same tension. Tension acts on both boxes but is internal to the system.

The clever trick: treat the two boxes + string as one system. Then internal forces (tension) can be ignored — only external forces matter. The only external horizontal force is F.

ACCELERATION OF THE WHOLE SYSTEMa = F ÷ (m₁ + m₂)

The system accelerates exactly like one single object of mass m₁ + m₂. The vertical forces — total weight (m₁ + m₂)·g downward and the normal force N₁ + N₂ upward from the ground — are balanced and do not affect horizontal motion.

🔄 Internal forces

Forces between objects inside the system (e.g. tension). They do not change the motion of the system as a whole.

🌍 External forces

Forces acting on the system from outside (e.g. the pull F). Only these change the system's motion.

💨Momentum — quantity of motion

MOMENTUM (p)p = mass × velocity = m · v  ·  vector, same direction as velocity

Momentum measures the “quantity of motion” of a body. A cricket ball and an iron ball rolling with the same velocity — the iron ball is harder to stop because its mass is larger. A slow truck and a fast car may need different forces to stop because momentum depends on both mass and velocity.

  • SI unit of momentum: kg m/s (or N·s).
  • Momentum is a vector — its direction is the direction of velocity.
  • Newton's second law in momentum form: net force = rate of change of momentum = (mv − mu)/t. This form works even when mass changes (like a rocket burning fuel).
  • Impulse = Force × time = change in momentum.

⚖️Conservation of momentum

LAW OF CONSERVATION OF MOMENTUMIf no EXTERNAL force acts on a system, its total momentum never changes — momentum is conserved

Internal forces (like a collision or an explosion inside the system) only transfer momentum between the parts — the total stays the same. For two objects A and B before and after a collision:

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

🎯 Famous examples

🔫 Recoil of a gun

Before firing, total momentum = 0. After firing, the bullet moves forward and the gun moves backward with equal momentum: 0 = M·V + m·v, so recoil velocity V = −(m/M)·v. (Solved example: a 5 kg rifle fires a 0.04 kg bullet at 200 m/s → recoil speed of rifle = 1.6 m/s backward.)

🚀 Rocket

Rocket + fuel = one system. Gases rush downward carrying momentum; the rocket must gain equal upward momentum — so it rises.

🪀 Colliding carts / balls

During any collision, whatever momentum one object loses, the other gains — the total is conserved.

🧠
Think: equal and opposite forces (third law) during a collision act for the same time, so the momentum changes are equal and opposite — that is why total momentum is conserved.

❓Ask & Understand (FAQ) — with Hinglish voice answers

System rules, momentum and conservation — with Hinglish voice answers.

?What are internal and external forces in a system of objects?🎧▾

Forces acting between the objects within a system are internal forces (e.g. the tension between two boxes connected by a string). Forces acting on the system from outside are external forces (e.g. the pull F). Only external forces can change the motion of the system as a whole.

?Two boxes of masses m₁ and m₂ are tied by a string and pulled with force F. What is the acceleration?🎧▾

Treat the two boxes and the string as one system. The internal tension is ignored. So a = F ÷ (m₁ + m₂). The system accelerates exactly like a single object of mass m₁ + m₂.

?What is momentum? Write its formula and unit.🎧▾

Momentum is the quantity of motion possessed by a body; it is the product of its mass and velocity: p = m·v. It is a vector quantity (direction = direction of velocity) and its SI unit is kg m/s.

?State the law of conservation of momentum. When does it apply?🎧▾

If no external force acts on a system, the total momentum of the system remains constant (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂). It applies whenever only internal forces act — collisions, firing of a gun, a rocket in space, an exploding firecracker.

?Why does a gun recoil when a bullet is fired? Find the recoil velocity formula.🎧▾

Before firing the system (gun + bullet) is at rest, so total momentum = 0. No external force acts during firing, so after firing the momenta must still add to zero: 0 = M·V + m·v. Hence the recoil velocity of the gun V = −(m/M)·v — negative sign shows it is backward. The heavier the gun, the smaller its recoil speed.

Press ▶ to listen. Explanation is in easy Hinglish; every science word is kept in English.
?How does a rocket move in empty space if there is nothing to push against?🎧▾

A rocket does not push against air. It ejects exhaust gases backward; by conservation of momentum, the rocket gains an equal forward momentum. This works perfectly in a vacuum — the rocket keeps accelerating as long as gases are expelled.

Press ▶ to listen. Explanation is in easy Hinglish; every science word is kept in English.
?A tractor pulls a harrow (mass m₁) with force F giving acceleration a₁, and a trolley (mass m₂) giving a₂. What is the acceleration if the trolley carries the harrow and is pulled with the same F?🎧▾

F = m₁a₁ and F = m₂a₂, so m₁ = F/a₁, m₂ = F/a₂. Combined mass = F/a₁ + F/a₂. New acceleration a = F/(F/a₁ + F/a₂) = a₁a₂/(a₁ + a₂). (Friction is ignored.)

📗Book Questions & Answers on this topic

Source questions for §6.7 Forces on a System + momentum from your notes.

1Short Answer: What are internal and external forces? Two boxes m₁ and m₂ are connected by a string and pulled by force F. Write the acceleration of the system.Book · Short Q12▾ Answer
Answer —

Forces between objects within the system = internal forces (tension T in the string). Forces from outside = external forces (the pull F). Only external forces change the motion of the whole system. Considering the boxes and string as one system, a = F/(m₁ + m₂) — it accelerates like a single object of mass m₁ + m₂.

2Notes (Solved Illustration): A rifle of mass 5 kg fires a bullet of mass 40 g at 200 m/s. Find (i) the recoil velocity of the rifle and (ii) the force of recoil if the bullet takes 0.004 s in the barrel.Solved on the Practice page▾ Answer
Answer —

This is Illustration 10 of your theory notes — its complete step-by-step solution is given on the 🧮 Solved Numericals Practice Zone page (linked in the menu). Quick check: recoil velocity V = −(m/M)·v = −(0.04/5)×200 = −1.6 m/s (backward).

3Bridging Science & Society: While walking, arms and legs move in a complex way, yet our motion can be studied by treating the body as one object. Why?NCERT · Beyond▾ Answer
Answer —

Because for studying overall motion, only the external forces on the body matter; the internal forces between arms, legs and torso cancel each other in pairs. So we can treat the whole body as a single system of total mass m and apply F = m·a to the net external force. Science often gets simpler when we look at the whole instead of every part!
