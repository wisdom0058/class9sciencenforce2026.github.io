🚀 6.6 Newton's Third Law of Motion

Every action has an equal and opposite reaction — walking, swimming, rockets and recoil.

🚀Newton's Third Law — forces always come in pairs

At least two objects must interact for a force to appear. Kick a ball — and you feel a force on your foot! Push a heavy table while sitting on a wheeled chair — the table pushes you back and your chair rolls away. This “two-way push” is summed up in the third law.

NEWTON'S THIRD LAW OF MOTIONWhenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object simultaneously exerts an EQUAL and OPPOSITE force on the first object.
⚠️
Very important: the two forces of the pair act on two different objects. That is why they do not cancel each other — forces can cancel only when they act on the same object.

🔬 Proof with two spring balances

Hook two identical spring balances together and pull. Whatever force you apply, both balances show the same reading — the two forces on each other are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

🏃The third law all around you

🚶 Walking

You push the ground backwards; friction pushes you forward. On ice (little friction) you slip — friction really does help us move.

🛶 Rowing a canoe

The paddle pushes water back; water pushes the paddle (and boat) forward. Push harder → bigger forward force → faster canoe.

🚀 Rocket & balloon

Gases rush down/back (action); they push the rocket/balloon up/forward (reaction). Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander fired engines to slow itself for a soft landing!

🔫 Gun recoil

The exploding gases push the bullet forward; the gun is pushed backward — that is the recoil you feel. Example 6.8: bullet 0.1 kg, gun 5 kg, force 2 N ⇒ abullet = 2/0.1 = 20 m/s², agun = 2/5 = 0.4 m/s². Equal forces but different accelerations (masses differ).

🌴 Climbing a tree

The climber's legs push the trunk down; friction pushes the climber up. Smooth trunks (less friction) are harder to climb.

🚒 Hose pipe

Water rushes out backward at high speed; the pipe is pushed forward — the fireperson has to hold it tightly!

🧲
Does the third law hold without contact? Yes! Two bar magnets attract/repel with equal-opposite magnetic forces; similarly charged balloons repel with equal-opposite electrostatic forces; and the Earth and a falling fruit pull each other with equal and opposite gravitational forces — even though none of them touch.

🌍Worked examples — Earth & fruit, bullet & gun

Example 6.7: The Earth and a fruit attract each other with equal and opposite gravitational forces. Then why does the fruit fall to the Earth and the Earth does not seem to move to the fruit?
The forces are equal, but acceleration = F/m. The Earth's mass is enormous (≈ 6 × 10²⁴ kg), so its acceleration a = F/MEarth is extremely small — too small to notice. The fruit's mass is tiny, so the same force gives it a large, visible acceleration.
Example 6.8: A 0.1 kg bullet is fired from a 5 kg gun with a force of 2 N. The gun recoils. Find the initial acceleration of the bullet and of the gun.
By the third law the recoil force on the gun is also 2 N (equal & opposite).
Acceleration of gun = F/m = 2/5 = 0.4 m/s².
Acceleration of bullet = F/m = 2/0.1 = 20 m/s².
Equal forces, but the accelerations differ because the masses differ.
🧠
Think it over: A canoe moves forward when the canoeist pushes water backward — that is the third law. And of two canoes (one empty, one carrying a passenger) pushed with the same paddle force, the empty canoe moves faster because it has less mass (second law: a = F/m).

?State Newton's third law of motion.🎧▾

Whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force on the first object. Forces always exist in pairs, and each pair acts on two different objects.

?The action and reaction forces are equal and opposite — why don't they cancel out?🎧▾

Because they act on two different objects. Forces can cancel only if they act on the same object. For example, when you push a wall, the wall pushes you back equally — but one force is on the wall and the other is on you, so nothing is cancelled and you both feel it.

?How does a rocket lift off from the ground?🎧▾

The rocket engine burns fuel and expels the exhaust gases downwards at high speed (action). By the third law, the gases exert an equal and opposite force upwards on the rocket (reaction). When this upward force is larger than the rocket's weight, the rocket lifts off.

?Give daily-life examples of Newton's third law.🎧▾

(1) Walking — push ground back, ground pushes you forward. (2) Rowing a boat — paddle pushes water back, water pushes boat forward. (3) Rocket/balloon — gases go one way, craft goes the other. (4) Gun recoil — bullet forward, gun backward. (5) A sailor jumping from a boat — boat moves backward. (6) Hose pipe — water rushes out, pipe gets pushed back and must be held tightly.

?A fruit and the Earth attract each other with equal forces. Why doesn't the Earth move up to the fruit?🎧▾

The forces are equal, but acceleration = force ÷ mass. The Earth's mass is about 6 × 10²⁴ kg — so its acceleration is unimaginably small (≈ 10⁻²³ m/s²). The fruit has a tiny mass, so it accelerates visibly towards the Earth.

?Does Newton's third law apply to non-contact forces like magnetic or gravitational forces?🎧▾

Yes. It applies to all forces. Two magnets exert equal and opposite magnetic forces on each other; similarly charged balloons repel with equal and opposite electrostatic forces; the Earth and a falling apple exert equal and opposite gravitational forces — all without touching.

?Why does a fireperson sometimes struggle to hold a pipe issuing water?🎧▾

Water rushes out of the nozzle with high speed (action). The pipe is pushed backward with an equal reaction force, so the fireperson has to push hard forward to keep the pipe steady.

?A spacecraft is where gravity is negligible. How can it change its velocity?🎧▾

It can fire its engines in the opposite direction to what it wants. The expelled gases push the spacecraft with an equal reaction force (third law) — this changes its velocity even in empty space. The Vikram lander of Chandrayaan-3 used exactly this to slow down for a soft landing near the Moon's south pole.

📗Book Questions & Answers on this topic

Pause-and-Ponder, Short and Long answers for §6.6.

1Pause and Ponder: Why does a fireperson sometimes struggle when holding the pipe issuing water?NCERT · P&P Q9▾ Answer
Answer —

When high-speed water rushes out of the pipe backward, by the third law the pipe experiences an equal and opposite reaction force pushing it forward. The fireperson must apply force to keep the pipe steady — that is why it is difficult to hold.

2Pause and Ponder: A spacecraft is moving where gravity is negligible. Suggest how it can change its velocity.NCERT · P&P Q10▾ Answer
Answer —

It can change its velocity by firing its engines: expel gases in one direction, and the gases push the spacecraft in the opposite direction with an equal force (third law). This works even where there is no air or gravity.

3Short Answer: State the third law. Using the example of a canoeist, explain why the two forces of the third law do not cancel.Book · Short Q10▾ Answer
Answer —

Third law: whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force on the first. A canoeist pushes water backward with the paddle; water pushes the paddle (and canoe) forward equally. These forces act on different objects (water vs paddle), so they cannot cancel. Only forces on the same object can cancel. The net forward force on the canoe accelerates it forward.

4Long Answer: State the third law. Explain four daily-life examples and why the Earth does not seem to move when a fruit falls towards it.Book · Long Q2▾ Answer
Answer —

Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction; the pair acts on two different objects. Examples: (1) Walking — foot pushes ground back, ground's friction pushes person forward. (2) Rowing a canoe — paddle pushes water back, water pushes paddle & canoe forward. (3) Rocket — exhaust gases go down, gases push rocket up. (4) Kicking a ball — foot pushes ball, ball pushes foot back (you feel the impact). Earth–fruit: the forces are equal, but a = F/m. Earth's mass (~6 × 10²⁴ kg) is so huge that Earth's acceleration is undetectable, while the fruit accelerates visibly towards the Earth.
