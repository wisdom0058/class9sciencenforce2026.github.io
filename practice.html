P1Illustration 1 · Momentum▾ Question & Solution
Question P1: A ball of mass 100 g is moving with a velocity of 15 m/s. Calculate the momentum associated with the ball.
Solution —

Given: m = 100 g = 0.1 kg  ·  v = 15 m/s  ·  Find: momentum p.

p = m·v = 0.1 × 15 = 1.5 kg m/s.  (Direction: same as the velocity.)

P2Illustration 2 · Force from F = ma▾ Question & Solution
Question P2: What force would be needed to produce an acceleration of 4.0 m/s² in a ball of mass 6 kg?
Solution —

Given: m = 6 kg, a = 4.0 m/s².

F = m·a = 6 × 4.0 = 24 N.

P3Illustration 3 · Short-time force▾ Question & Solution
Question P3: A force acts for 0.2 s on a body of mass 2.5 kg initially at rest. The force then ceases to act and the body moves through 4 m in the next one second. Calculate the magnitude of the force.
Solution —

When the force stops acting, the body moves with constant velocity. It covers 4 m in 1 s, so this uniform velocity = 4/1 = 4 m/s. The force produced this velocity from rest in 0.2 s:

a = (v − u)/t = (4 − 0)/0.2 = 20 m/s².
Force F = m·a = 2.5 × 20 = 50 N.

P4Illustration 4 · Momentum & force (all parts)▾ Question & Solution
Question P4: A ball of mass 20 g is initially moving with a velocity of 100 m/s. On applying a constant force for 0.5 s, it acquires a velocity of 150 m/s. Calculate: (i) initial momentum, (ii) final momentum, (iii) rate of change of momentum, (iv) acceleration, (v) magnitude of the force applied.
Solution —

Given: m = 20 g = 0.02 kg, u = 100 m/s, v = 150 m/s, t = 0.5 s.

(i) Initial momentum p₁ = m·u = 0.02 × 100 = 2 kg m/s.
(ii) Final momentum p₂ = m·v = 0.02 × 150 = 3 kg m/s.
(iii) Rate of change of momentum = (p₂ − p₁)/t = (3 − 2)/0.5 = 2 N.
(iv) Acceleration a = (v − u)/t = (150 − 100)/0.5 = 100 m/s².
(v) Force F = m·a = 0.02 × 100 = 2 N.

P5Illustration 5 · Braking force▾ Question & Solution
Question P5: A motorcycle is moving with a velocity of 108 km/h and it takes 5 s to stop after the brakes are applied. Calculate the force exerted by the brakes if its mass along with the rider is 250 kg.
Solution —

Given: u = 108 km/h = 108 × (5/18) = 30 m/s; v = 0; t = 5 s; m = 250 kg.

a = (v − u)/t = (0 − 30)/5 = −6 m/s² (deceleration).
Braking force = m·a = 250 × 6 = 1500 N opposite to the motion (magnitude 1500 N).

P6Illustration 6 · Ratio of two forces▾ Question & Solution
Question P6: A force F₁ acting on a body of 2 kg produces an acceleration of 2.5 m/s². Another force F₂ acting on a body of mass 5 kg produces an acceleration of 2 m/s². Find the ratio F₂ : F₁.
Solution —

F₁ = m·a = 2 × 2.5 = 5 N.
F₂ = m·a = 5 × 2 = 10 N.

Ratio F₂/F₁ = 10/5 = 2, i.e. F₂ : F₁ = 2 : 1.

P7Illustration 7 · Same force, tied masses▾ Question & Solution
Question P7: A force of 20 N acting on a mass m₁ produces an acceleration of 4 m/s². The same force applied on mass m₂ produces 0.5 m/s². What acceleration would the same force produce when both masses are tied together?
Solution —

m₁ = F/a = 20/4 = 5 kg.
m₂ = F/a = 20/0.5 = 40 kg.

Tied together, total mass = 5 + 40 = 45 kg.
a = F/m = 20/45 = 0.44 m/s².

P8Illustration 8 · Car hitting a wall▾ Question & Solution
Question P8: A car of mass 1000 kg moving with a velocity of 36 km/h hits a wall and comes to rest in 5 s. Find the force exerted by the car on the wall.
Solution —

Given: m = 1000 kg, u = 36 km/h = 10 m/s, v = 0, t = 5 s.

F = m(v − u)/t = 1000 × (0 − 10)/5 = −2000 N.
The magnitude of the force exerted by the car on the wall = 2000 N. (By the third law the wall pushes the car back with an equal force — that is what stops the car.)

P9Illustration 9 · Rebound momentum change▾ Question & Solution
Question P9: When a ball of mass m kg strikes a wall with a speed u m/s and rebounds at the same speed, find the change in momentum.
Solution —

Take the initial direction as positive: p₁ = +mu.
After rebound the ball moves in the opposite direction: p₂ = −mu.

Change in momentum = p₂ − p₁ = −mu − mu = −2mu kg m/s.
Magnitude is 2mu — the ball's momentum completely reverses.

P10Illustration 10 · Recoil of a rifle▾ Question & Solution
Question P10: A rifle of mass 5 kg fires a bullet of mass 40 g. The bullet leaves the barrel with a velocity of 200 m/s. If the bullet takes 0.004 s to move through the barrel, calculate: (i) the recoil velocity of the rifle and (ii) the force experienced by the rifle due to its recoil.
Solution —

Given: M = 5 kg, m = 40 g = 0.04 kg, v = 200 m/s, t = 0.004 s.

(i) Conservation of momentum: before firing total momentum = 0, so 0 = M·V + m·v.
V = −(m/M)·v = −(0.04/5) × 200 = −1.6 m/s (the rifle recoils backward at 1.6 m/s).

(ii) Force = change in momentum of rifle ÷ time = (0 − 5 × (−1.6))/0.004 = 8/0.004 = 2000 N backward. (The bullet is pushed forward by an equal 2000 N.)

P11Illustration 11 · Machine gun▾ Question & Solution
Question P11: A machine gun has a mass of 30 kg. It fires 35 g bullets at the rate of 400 bullets per minute with a speed of 400 m/s. What force must be applied to the gun so that it is kept in position?
Solution —

Given: M = 30 kg, m = 35 g = 0.035 kg, v = 400 m/s, rate = 400/min.

Bullets fired per second, n = 400/60 = 20/3.
Force needed = rate of change of momentum of the bullets = n·m·v = (20/3) × 0.035 × 400 = 93.3 N.
The gunner must push the gun forward with about 93.3 N to stop it from moving back.

P12Book Long-Answer Q5 · Bullet in a wooden block▾ Question & Solution
Question P12: A bullet of mass 50 g is moving with a speed of 100 m/s and enters a heavy stationary wooden block. It stops after penetrating a distance of 50 cm. Estimate the stopping force acting on the bullet (assume constant acceleration). Also explain, using Newton's third law, what force the bullet exerts on the block, and why the bullet does not come back out.
Solution —

m = 50 g = 0.05 kg, u = 100 m/s, v = 0, s = 50 cm = 0.5 m.

Step 1 — deceleration: v² = u² + 2as ⇒ 0 = 100² + 2·a·0.5 ⇒ a = −10,000 m/s².

Step 2 — stopping force: F = m·a = 0.05 × 10,000 = 500 N backward on the bullet.

Third law: the bullet pushes the block forward with an equal 500 N, but the block is heavy so its acceleration is very small. The bullet does not come back out because once its velocity reaches zero there is no net forward force left on it, and the wood grips the bullet on all sides.

T1A 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s is stopped in 0.5 s. What force is needed?▾ Answer
Answer — F = m(v − u)/t = 2 × (0 − 5)/0.5 = −20 N (20 N opposite to motion).
T2Find the weight of a 12 kg object near the Earth (g = 10 m/s²).▾ Answer
Answer — F = mg = 12 × 10 = 120 N.
T3A 0.2 kg ball moving at 4 m/s collides with a stationary 0.2 kg ball and stops. What is the speed of the second ball after collision (no external force)?▾ Answer
Answer — momentum conserved: 0.2×4 = 0.2×v ⇒ v = 4 m/s (equal masses simply swap velocity).
T4Two forces 8 N and 15 N act on a block in opposite directions. Mass of block = 3.5 kg. Find its acceleration.▾ Answer
Answer — net force = 15 − 8 = 7 N; a = F/m = 7/3.5 = 2 m/s² in the direction of the 15 N force.
