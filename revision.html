📌Chapter 6 — At a Glance (from your book)

  • The force of friction acts on an object in the direction opposite to its motion.
  • Newton's first law: an object at rest stays at rest, and a moving object keeps moving with constant velocity, unless a net force acts on it.
  • Newton's second law: when a net force acts, the object accelerates in the direction of the net force; a ∝ F and a ∝ 1/m (so F = ma).
  • Newton's third law: whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force on the first.

🔗 System

a = F ÷ (m₁ + m₂)

🔫 Recoil

👨‍🔬Meet the scientists & the journey beyond

🇮🇹 Galileo Galilei (17th c.)

Thought experiments: on a frictionless horizontal surface a body moves forever at constant velocity.

🇬🇧 Isaac Newton (1687)

Named 'inertia', framed the three laws, and got the unit of force named after him (newton, N).

🚀
The Journey Beyond: Newton's laws work across an enormous range — from dust particles to planets and stars. They need modification only very close to massive objects (strong gravity), at speeds near the speed of light, and at very small (atomic) scales, where modern physics (Einstein's relativity and quantum mechanics) takes over.

🎯 Quests you can try (from your book)

  • Does friction depend on how hard surfaces press each other? Is friction just before moving bigger than after motion begins? Is rolling friction less than sliding friction? Make an infographic.
  • Two toy cars with touching like poles of magnets — do they travel equal distances in opposite directions? Add equal masses and repeat. Plot distance vs mass.
  • Wrap a rope around a rough post and hold a heavy bucket — each extra turn increases the grip much more than you'd expect!
  • Read excerpts from Newton's Principia to see how he actually framed the laws.
