✅Exercise Q1 – Q8 · with solutions

1Using a horizontal force F, a table is moved across the floor at a constant velocity. How much is the frictional force exerted by the floor on the table?▾ Show full answer
Answer —

Since the table moves with constant velocity, its acceleration is zero. By Newton's first law, the net force on it must be zero. Therefore the frictional force of the floor must be exactly equal to F, acting in the direction opposite to the motion.

2For a ball moving on a smooth frictionless surface, choose the correct option: (i) If no net force is applied on the ball, its velocity will remain the same / increase / decrease. (ii) If a net force is applied in the direction of its motion, the speed will remain the same / increase / decrease. (iii) If a net force is applied opposite to the motion, the speed will remain the same / increase / decrease.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

(i) remain the same — zero net force ⇒ no change in velocity (first law).
(ii) increase — net force along the motion accelerates it, speed rises.
(iii) decrease — net force opposite to motion decelerates it, speed falls (and it may stop or reverse).

3Two blocks P and Q are on a smooth horizontal surface. Forces of 4 N and 5 N act in opposite directions on block P, while block Q is moving with a constant velocity. Which statement is correct? (i) P experiences a net force and Q does not. (ii) P does not and Q does. (iii) Both experience a net force. (iv) Neither experiences a net force.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

(i) P experiences a net force and Q does not experience a net force.
On P the two opposite forces are unequal (5 N and 4 N), so net force on P = 1 N (towards the 5 N force). Q moves with constant velocity ⇒ acceleration 0 ⇒ net force on Q is zero.

4In a snake-boat race (Vallam kali, Kerala) 100 oarsmen row together — 95 row backward to propel the boat forward, but 5 mistakenly row in the opposite direction. Each oarsman applies 200 N. What is the net force on the boat?▾ Show full answer
Answer —

Forward force = 95 × 200 = 19,000 N.
Backward force = 5 × 200 = 1,000 N.
Net force = 19,000 − 1,000 = 18,000 N in the forward direction.

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
5When a net force acts on an object, the object accelerates — (i) opposite to the force with a ∝ force, (ii) opposite to the force with a ∝ mass, (iii) in the direction of force with a ∝ 1/force, (iv) in the direction of force with a ∝ force.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

(iv) in the direction of force, with acceleration proportional to the force acting on the object.
By the second law, acceleration is in the direction of the net force, a = F/m — directly proportional to F (for fixed m) and inversely proportional to m (for fixed F).

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
6The position-time graphs for four objects A, B, C and D moving along a straight line are given. A net force acts on: (i) A (ii) B (iii) C (iv) D.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

A net force acts only on an object whose velocity is changing (acceleration ≠ 0). In a position–time graph:
horizontal line ⇒ at rest (no net force); straight inclined line ⇒ constant velocity (no net force); curved graph ⇒ velocity is changing ⇒ acceleration ⇒ net force.
Hence a net force acts on (iii) Object C and (iv) Object D (their graphs are curved).

7A sailor jumps out from a small boat to the shore. As the sailor jumps forward, will the boat move? If yes, in which direction and why?▾ Show full answer
Answer —

Yes, the boat moves backward. When the sailor pushes the boat backward while jumping forward (action), by Newton's third law the boat pushes the sailor forward with an equal force (reaction) — and the boat itself moves backward.

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
8During a high-jump event, a landing mat or sand bed is placed for the athlete to fall upon. Explain the reason.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

When the athlete lands on a soft mat or sand, the stopping time of the fall becomes larger. For the same change in velocity (momentum), a longer time means a much smaller force on the body (F = m·Δv/Δt). A hard ground would stop the athlete almost instantly, producing an enormous force and serious injury.

✅Exercise Q9 – Q16 · with solutions

9A loaded hand cart collides with an identical but empty hand cart. During the collision: (i) loaded cart exerts a larger force, (ii) empty cart exerts a larger force, (iii) neither exerts a force, (iv) both exert equal forces on each other.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

(iv) the loaded cart and the empty cart both exert an equal magnitude of force on each other.
By Newton's third law, forces in a collision always come in equal-and-opposite pairs, whatever the masses may be.

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
10The acceleration-mass graph for the acceleration produced by a force on objects of different masses is given. Plot the force-mass graph for this case.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

From the second law, F = m·a. The acceleration–mass graph shows a hyperbolic curve (a = F/m), which means the same force F was applied throughout — the product m·a is constant.
Therefore the force–mass graph is a horizontal straight line parallel to the mass axis at the constant force value (F = 10 N for the figure).

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
11The velocity-time graph of an object of mass 10 kg moving along a straight line is given (Fig. 6.41). Calculate the force acting on the object using the graph.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

From the graph: the object's velocity increases from u = 10 m/s at t = 0 s to v = 30 m/s at t = 8 s.
Acceleration a = (v − u)/t = (30 − 10)/8 = 20/8 = 2.5 m/s².
Force F = m·a = 10 kg × 2.5 m/s² = 25 N.
(Method: read any two points on the straight v–t line; slope of the line = acceleration, then use F = ma.)

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
12A bullet of mass 50 g moving at 100 m/s enters a heavy stationary wooden block and stops after penetrating 50 cm. Estimate the stopping force on the bullet.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

m = 50 g = 0.05 kg, u = 100 m/s, v = 0, s = 50 cm = 0.5 m.
Using v² = u² + 2as: 0 = (100)² + 2·a·0.5 ⇒ 0 = 10,000 + a ⇒ a = −10,000 m/s² (deceleration).
Stopping force F = m·a = 0.05 × 10,000 = 500 N, acting opposite to the direction of motion.

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
13An ace footballer kicked the football with a speed of 108 km/h, imparting an estimated force of 800 N. The ball's mass is 0.4 kg. Calculate the time of contact between foot and ball.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

Speed: 108 km/h = 108 × (1000/3600) = 30 m/s. Ball starts from rest: u = 0, v = 30 m/s.
Using F = m(v − u)/t: 800 = 0.4 × (30 − 0)/t ⇒ 800·t = 12 ⇒ t = 12/800 = 0.015 s.
The foot stays in contact with the ball for just 15 thousandths of a second!

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
14An object of mass 2 kg moving at a constant 10 m/s enters a rough patch where friction is 7 N; at the same time an additional 3 N opposing force is applied. How far does it travel before coming to rest?▾ Show full answer
Answer —

Total opposing force = 7 + 3 = 10 N.
Deceleration a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s² (opposite to motion, take a = −5 m/s²).
Using v² = u² + 2as: 0 = (10)² + 2(−5)·s ⇒ 0 = 100 − 10s ⇒ s = 10 m.

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
15A tractor pulls a harrow (mass m₁) with net force F giving acceleration a₁; the same tractor pulls a trolley (mass m₂) with F giving a₂. If the tractor pulls the trolley with the harrow placed on it (same force F), find the resulting acceleration in terms of a₁ and a₂.▾ Show full answer
Answer —

For the harrow: F = m₁a₁ ⇒ m₁ = F/a₁.
For the trolley: F = m₂a₂ ⇒ m₂ = F/a₂.
Combined mass = m₁ + m₂ = F/a₁ + F/a₂ = F(a₁ + a₂)/(a₁a₂).
Acceleration of the combined system a = F/(m₁ + m₂) = F ÷ [F(a₁ + a₂)/(a₁a₂)] = a₁a₂/(a₁ + a₂).

Step-by-step solution explained slowly in Hinglish (science words in English).
16When a bar-magnet pole is brought near a compass, the magnet and the compass needle (a magnet) exert equal and opposite forces on each other (third law). Yet only the needle moves. Why?▾ Show full answer
Answer —

By the third law both feel equal and opposite forces. But from the second law, a = F/m — acceleration depends on mass. The compass needle has a very small mass, so the same force gives it a large, visible acceleration. The bar magnet has a very large mass, so its acceleration is extremely small — too small to be observed.
